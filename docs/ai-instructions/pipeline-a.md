# Pipeline A instructions

## Purpose
Pipeline A covers research, ideation, scripting, repurposing, and distribution for content operations.

## Research and ideation
When asked to research topics or generate video ideas:
1. Search for current real estate or mortgage questions that appear to have strong audience interest.
2. Look for signals from search behavior, community discussions, and existing content patterns.
3. Output ideas as a ranked list with:
   - title idea
   - why it is timely
   - search angle
   - content type such as explainer, news, or case study
4. Flag topics that depend on rate data or other time-sensitive information that should be verified before production.

## Scripting
When writing a script:
- Use a scene-by-scene structure with clear markers such as HOOK, INTRO, SECTION_1, and CTA.
- Make the hook land within the first 15 seconds.
- Keep long-form scripts around 6–10 minutes and short-form scripts around 45–60 seconds.
- Add inline cues for visual support where needed.
- Add graphic cues for numbers, comparisons, or processes that benefit from visuals.
- End with one clear call to action.
- After writing, include a short description and a set of tags.

## Script checklist
- Hook lands early.
- Disclaimer is included.
- Time-sensitive data is flagged when relevant.
- Jargon is defined or avoided.
- The call to action is singular and clear.

## Repurposing
When given a video URL or transcript:
1. Extract the strongest standalone points.
2. Produce platform-native assets such as a LinkedIn post, a short X/Twitter thread, a short-form script, and a newsletter section.
3. Make each asset feel native to its platform rather than copied verbatim.
4. Tailor tone and structure to the platform.

## Distribution
When scheduling content:
- Prefer Tuesday or Thursday for long-form YouTube content.
- Keep short-form distribution frequent if the pipeline allows.
- Avoid scheduling more than two pieces of content on the same platform on the same day.
- Confirm the schedule before posting.

## Analytics feedback loop
When reviewing analytics:
1. Flag videos with a large drop-off in the first 30 seconds.
2. Identify videos with unusually strong retention and describe what likely contributed to that performance.
3. Summarize top-performing content by views, watch time, and discovery terms.
4. Feed successful patterns back into future scripting decisions.
